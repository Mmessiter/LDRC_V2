A mix makes one channel (the slave) follow another (the master):|
rudder that follows the ailerons, say.||
TO SET UP A MIX:|
1. Choose a 'Mix number' with its '-' and '+'.|
2. Turn on 'Mix input' or 'Mix output' (see below). With both|
off, the mix does nothing.|
3. Set 'Bank (0 = all)', 'Master channel' and 'Slave channel'.|
Their names show on the right.|
4. Set 'Percent': how much of the master's movement the slave|
gets (100 = all of it).|
5. Press 'OK' to save.|
Each '-' or '+' works at once: watch the servos as you go.||
MIX INPUT OR MIX OUTPUT:|
'Mix input' adds the master's stick movement to the slave's|
stick, before the slave's curve.|
The slave's own curve then shapes the result.|
'Mix output' adds the master's output (after its own curve)|
to the slave's output.|
Either way, the slave still follows its own stick or knob|
through its own curve.|
It never goes past that curve's end points.||
MASTERS AND SLAVES:|
A master can have many slaves; a slave can have many masters.|
A slave can even have its own master as its slave, as V tails,|
flaperons and delta wings need. Use 'Mix output' for these.|
Output mixes do not pass anything on: each one takes the master's|
own output, never what another output mix added to it.|
Input mixes work in 'Mix number' order, so a later input mix sees|
what an earlier one did.||
ONE DIRECTION AND REVERSED:|
'One direction' moves the slave one way only, whichever way the|
master moves: up elevator with left AND right rudder, say.|
'Reversed' moves the slave the other way.|
With 'One direction' on, 'Reversed' chooses which way.||
OFFSET:|
Use 'Offset' when the master does not rest at its centre,|
as with a throttle.|
Try values until the slave is centred with the master at rest.|
If you change 'Percent', check 'Offset' again: the value|
needed changes with it.|
