GPS: where the model is, how fast and how high.|
It needs a GPS (GNSS) sensor in the model.||
'Mark here' remembers where the model is now. From then on,|
'Yards to mark', 'Heading to mark' and 'Max distance (yards)'|
are measured from there, and the maximum starts again from zero.|
Mark the take-off spot before you fly: the page then tells you|
how far the model went, and which way is back.||
'Zero alt' makes the present altitude zero.|
Press it with the model on the ground: the altitudes then show|
the height above that spot. 'Max altitude (feet)' starts again.|
Until you press it, they are above mean sea level.||
'Telemetry ...' goes back to the Telemetry page.||
THE SENSOR:|
A PA1010D GNSS sensor on an I2C breakout board (address 0x10),|
wired to the I2C port of a Version 1 receiver.|
The V2 receiver does not send GPS data.|
The first fix can take a few minutes, so be patient.|
'GPS fix' says Yes once it has one.|
Accuracy improves with the number of satellites found.|
Location is within about 2 metres in good conditions.|
In poor conditions it is less accurate.||
UNITS:|
Distances are in yards, speeds in mph, altitudes in feet.|
Headings are in degrees, clockwise from due north.|
'Heading' is the way the model is moving.|
'Heading to mark' is the way from the model back to the mark.||
CLOCK:|
The satellites carry very accurate atomic clocks, so the|
transmitter's clock is set from them automatically.|
