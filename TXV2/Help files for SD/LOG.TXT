Read the transmitter's log: what happened each day it was used.|
'Log' on 'Model setup' opens today's log, named at the top.||
TO MOVE AROUND:|
Drag the text up or down to scroll.|
'Top' and 'Bottom' jump to the start and the end.|
'Files' lists all the log files, to view or delete them. With a|
model connected it says 'Switch it off to see the files.'|
No log yet today? Then it says 'File not found'.|
Press 'Files' to read another day.|
'OK' goes to the 'Telemetry' page.||
WHAT IT RECORDS:|
Connections, disconnections and gaps in the radio link.|
Which model memory was found, and bank and rate changes.|
The motor and safety switches, and the buddy switch.|
How long the motor ran, battery voltages and warnings.|
The highest head speed, current and temperature, and mAh used.|
Each event is marked with the time.||
GAPS:|
A gap is when data packets fail to arrive, through interference|
or great distance. The system reconnects as fast as possible.|
Gaps are measured in milliseconds (ms).|
Short gaps, up to 100 ms or so, are not noticed by most pilots.|
Longer ones might point to a problem.||
SETTINGS:|
On 'Transmitter setup', 'System', then 'Next >' twice (Advanced):|
'Keep log files' switches logging on or off.|
'Log receiver radio swaps' records when the receiver swaps|
between its radios (transceivers).|
'Log gaps longer than' sets the shortest gap logged (in ms).||
THE SD CARD:|
Log files are kept on the transmitter's SD card.|
A new file is started each day, named by the date.|
SD cards only last a year or so; they last longer when rarely|
written to. So switch logging off when you do not need it,|
and keep a copy of the card's files on your computer.|
