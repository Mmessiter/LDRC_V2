Telemetry: how well the radio link is working, and what the|
model reports. Look at it after a flight, before you switch off.||
'Clear' sets the counts back to zero: lost packets, the comms|
gaps (and the 'Gaps ...' page), the frame rates, and the GPS|
page's maximum speed and distance.|
They also start again a few seconds after each connection, and|
when you press 'OK' on the 'Rotorflight' page.|
'Gaps ...' shows how long each reconnection took.|
'GPS ...' opens the GPS page.||
THE LINK:|
Average and current frame rate: packets received each second.|
Lost data packets: packets that did not arrive.|
Longest comms gap: the most milliseconds between losing a data|
packet and being connected again. Below about 250 (a quarter of|
a second) all should be well; normally it is well below 100.|
If it is much higher, move the receiver's aerials, and check|
them for damage. Dipole aerials are recommended.|
Average comms gap: the average length of the gaps (ms).|
Packets received: as counted by the receiver.||
TRANSCEIVERS:|
Receiver type: TRX is how many transceivers it has|
(TRX:2 = two). V2 means a Version 2 receiver.|
PWM:8 or PWM:11: the servo outputs of a Version 1 receiver.|
Transceiver swaps: how often the receiver changed over from|
one transceiver to another.|
Transceiver 1, 2 and 3 time: how long each has been in use.||
THE MODEL:|
Max head speed, Max ESC temp and ESC temp come from Rotorflight.|
RX volts per cell: the model's battery.|
TX volts per cell: the transmitter's battery.|
RX and TX firmware: the version numbers.||
ALTITUDE:|
Altitude is not shown on this page.|
A Version 1 receiver with a BMP280 pressure sensor measures it.|
With 'Keep log files' on, the highest altitude goes into the log|
when the model disconnects.|
The sensor needs the local sea-level pressure (QNH).|
Enter it in 'Sea-level pressure (QNH)', on page 3 of|
Transmitter setup / 'System'. A nearby airfield's web site,|
or an AI assistant, will tell you.|
To measure from where you stand, press 'Zero alt' on the GPS|
page with the model on the ground.|
Pressure altitude is usually right to within a foot or so,|
far better than altitude from GPS.|
