HOW TO REVIEW THIS APP WITHOUT THE HARDWARE

This app configures a model-helicopter receiver that I design and build myself,
so you will not have one. You do not need one.

The app opens on a screen of four buttons: Connect, Reviews, Backups, Demos.

    Tap DEMOS.

It offers three demos — Receiver, Rotorflight dongle, Simulator interface.
Tap any of them. The whole app then runs against a simulated helicopter, with
every page live and editable. That is the intended way to review it, and it
needs no hardware, no account and no sign-in. Bluetooth may be denied and the
demos still work.

Only Connect needs hardware; every other button, including the demos, works
on its own. A ? on every screen opens its help.

BLUETOOTH, AND WHY IT RUNS IN THE BACKGROUND (guideline 2.5.4)
Everything the app does with real hardware goes over Bluetooth Low Energy,
using Core Bluetooth: it scans for the receiver's GATT service, connects, and
exchanges JSON with it over two characteristics. In the demo there is no
Bluetooth traffic at all, which is why a review without the hardware sees
none - a screen recording of the app driving a real receiver is here:

    https://messiter.com/rxv2/review/bluetooth.mp4

It shows: the scan finding a receiver by name and signal strength, a connection,
Rotorflight pages reading live values over the link, and a firmware update
being sent to the receiver over Bluetooth.

The bluetooth-central background mode exists for that last step. A firmware
update is several megabytes pushed to the receiver over BLE and takes a few
minutes. If the user switches to another app mid-transfer, the mode keeps the
transfer alive instead of abandoning the receiver half-written; and when the
receiver reboots into the new firmware and the link drops, the mode lets the
app be woken to reconnect and confirm the version. That is the whole use - the
app does no scanning, tracking or beaconing in the background, and drops any
link after a minute away in normal use.

WHAT IT IS
The LDRC RX V2 is a radio-control receiver for model helicopters. The app talks
to it over Bluetooth LE using a standard GATT service — no MFi accessory, no
External Accessory framework. It sets flight-controller parameters, reads flight
logs, and can send the receiver a firmware update.

ABOUT THE WEB VIEW (guideline 4.2 and 2.5.2)
Part of the interface is a web view. Every page is BUNDLED INSIDE THIS BINARY
(webroot/); no HTML or JavaScript is ever fetched at run time, and there is no
server. Only JSON crosses Bluetooth, to and from the user's own receiver.
Firmware images download over HTTPS from messiter.com only (host hard-coded)
and go to the receiver; they never execute on iOS. Native, outside the web
view: the Bluetooth scanner, model photographs, backup files via the share
sheet and Files, and offline replay of a recorded session.

SAFETY
The app writes stored settings only. It cannot arm the model, spin the motor or
move a control surface in real time, and it refuses to change anything while the
flight controller reports itself armed.

PRIVACY
No accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no data collection whatsoever. Settings
and photographs stay in the app's own storage on the phone.

A "Send debug data to Malcolm" button gathers the receiver's own event log and
settings (no passwords, no WiFi credentials, nothing about the phone's owner)
into a text file and hands it to the system share sheet. The user chooses where
it goes; the app never transmits it by itself.
