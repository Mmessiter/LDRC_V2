With a USB cable to the flight controller, D5 and D6 carry nothing — the dongle uses USB and falls back to these only if USB does not come up. Worth wiring anyway: they cost nothing and they are the safety net. Link above says which one is in use.

In Rotorflight (Ports), set that UART to MSP at the speed below. The receiver port stays with whatever receiver flies the model.

Take the 5 V from the flight controller, not from a separate supply — see the warning below if you also use a USB cable.

With a USB cable to the flight controller, power the dongle from the flight controller’s own 5 V.

The dongle drives the USB cable, so it puts 5 V down it — and most flight controllers feed USB power into their own 5 V rail. A separate supply would therefore meet the flight controller’s own 5 V through that cable. No two supplies are the same voltage, so one pushes current into the other; and with the flight pack switched off the dongle would power the whole flight controller through the cable, which is how servos come half alive and twitch. Taking the 5 V from the flight controller keeps it all one rail.

A separate supply is only safe with the USB cable unplugged.
